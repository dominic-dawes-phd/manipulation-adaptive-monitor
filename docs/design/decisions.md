# Design decisions

Dated entries, newest first. Each entry states what was chosen, what was rejected and why. **PROPOSED** entries need the user's confirmation before they produce reported results.

## Nov 26, 2026 (after M2: next steps and the trigger signal check)

The user approved the plan for M3 onward: a trigger signal check first, then Q7/Q8, the VLM gate (M3), the world model (M4), and M5.

- **D-21 The disagreement signal exists for transfer_cube, weakly for insertion (dev check, provisional).** `scripts/trigger_signal_check.py` compares d(t) in the 20 steps after each perturbation with the same steps of the same dev seed run without it (identical action sequences up to the perturbation in 46/46). Sigma is from the M1 baseline successes and the percentile reference from all unperturbed steps, both standing in for calib. Crossing the 0.99 percentile, perturbed vs. unperturbed same steps: nudge 8/8 vs 0/8 (1 step after the nudge, 19 before the `grasp_miss` onset); slip 9/10 vs 3/10 (median 6 steps; onset median 4); knock 6/10 vs 3/10 (median 8; onset median 9); insertion knock 2/9 vs 0/9 (0.95: 5/9 vs 1/9); peg_misalign 0/9 vs 0/9. Observation: perturbations fire during transport and handover, where unperturbed disagreement already sits near the 0.85-0.95 percentile, so a single global threshold separates poorly at 0.95. A step-conditioned percentile F̂_calib(d | t), which is causal, is a candidate (PROPOSED, decide in M5 on calib only). Consequences: H2's knock and slip claims stay testable; the insertion results will mostly rest on the max-gap timeout and gripper flips, and peg_misalign is expected to be a hard case for every policy-derived trigger. Not reported; the main results use calib sigma and test episodes.
- **D-23 Q7 and Q8 implemented (ACCEPTED, 1.0.00).** As accepted on Nov 25, with one precision: "before the perturbation" means onset ≤ `perturb_step`, because frame `perturb_step` is rendered before the perturbed action (D-17). The perturbation name serves as the onset source, so no extra field is stored. Applied to the review episodes as a preview (not stored; finished runs are not edited): 11 of 50 relabeled (CHANGELOG 1.0.00). Two of them were late sim labels on episodes that did fail from the perturbation (nudge seed 2009, drop 56 steps after the nudge; knock seed 2002, `handover_miss` 163 steps after), which the 50-step window now attributes to the perturbation. Rejected: leaving them `unlabeled` (no delay for 7/10 peg_misalign failures). The 50-step window is the accepted value; a longer one would label nudge seed 2009 by its drop at step 203 instead.
- **D-22 All reported episodes come from the 8x RTX 3070 server (ACCEPTED, user).** The laptop is for development and dev-split checks. Before the pilot, the server must reproduce a seed exactly twice and run the tests; D-09 forbids pooling with laptop runs.

## Nov 25, 2026 (M2, after the user's answers)

The user accepted D-11, D-12 (for the laptop), D-13 and D-15, the label fixes of Q5 and Q6, and the insertion scope of Q4, and asked for a gentler knock (Q3) and a runner for the 8x RTX 3070 server.

- **D-16 Label definitions (ACCEPTED: Q5, Q6).** *Held*: either finger of the arm touches the object and its center is more than `hold_margin_m` = 2 cm above `rest_height_m`. This clears the resting center (cube 2.00 cm, peg 0.99 cm, socket 2.19 cm) and a cube tipped onto a corner (+1.46 cm). *Drop*: an object held by some arm is then held by no arm for `drop_debounce_steps` = 5 steps; the onset is the first step without a hold. In successful baseline replays, firm grasps lost contact for 1 step 58 times and for 2 steps 6 times, so a debounce is needed. A handover from arm to arm is not a drop. *handover_miss*: stage 3 or higher is not reached within `handover_window` steps of the first stage 2, raised from 120 to 200 because successful handovers take 77-181 steps (median 142, 79 episodes); with 120, most good handovers would have been labeled as failures. When a drop and a timeout coincide, the earliest onset wins; the spec's "or the cube drops during the handover window" case is labeled `drop`. Rejected: a hold test on height alone, which cannot tell a lifted cube from a cube pushed against the table.
- **D-17 Frame alignment and episode store (ACCEPTED, refines spec section 2).** Every per-step array except `reward` describes frame t, before action t; `reward[t]` is the outcome of action t; `phase[t]` = max(reward[:t]), so boundaries are steps at which a check could first see the new stage. `perturb_step` is the action step during which the physics were first changed, so its effect is visible from frame `perturb_step + 1`. Added keys: `contact` and `held` [T, K, 2] (arms ordered left, right). Rendering now draws only the top camera (gym-aloha drew three and returned one); the M1 episodes are reproduced bit for bit by `scripts/verify_against_baseline.py` (10/10), at 12.7 s instead of 17 s per transfer_cube episode. New split `dev` = seeds 2000-2199, for review videos and tuning, never reported. `calib` widened to 10000-10999 because the main sweep asks for 750 insertion calib episodes.
- **D-18 Knock = a 5 N horizontal push that stops when the fingers lose the object (ACCEPTED: user asked for "slip out, bounce or roll once at most").** Measured by replaying recorded baseline actions (40 successful episodes per setting, `scripts/calibrate_perturbations.py`, runs `perturb_calibration_20261125-165042` and `knock_release_20261125-170035`). A push of fixed duration is all or nothing: at 5 N for 3 steps 2.5% of cubes drop; at 8 N 52% drop but 40% leave the table at up to 12 m/s; at the specified 20 N, 80% drop and 65% leave the table at up to 24 m/s, because the force keeps accelerating a 50 g cube after the grip breaks. The push is therefore checked at every 2 ms physics substep and stops after two substeps without finger contact, or after `steps` = 10 control steps. At 5 N with a random direction: 42% of transfer_cube knocks drop the cube within 60 steps (median 7 steps after the push starts), 2.5% leave the table, median travel 15 cm (max 36 cm), 2.5% bounce more than once; 30% of knocked episodes still succeed. At 6 N, 77% drop but 15% leave the table (max 9.5 m/s, cubes squeezed out of the fingers). Insertion at 5 N: 47% of pegs drop, none leave the table. Rejected: pushing across the finger closing axis (30% drop on the cube), and higher forces (fling cases). Consequence: only about 40% of knock episodes contain a drop, so the main sweep needs more knock episodes for the same number of labeled drops (DOE section 5). Knocks that shift the object without a drop and still cause a failure get a late `handover_miss` or `insert_miss` label; review counts them.
- **D-19 Slip, nudge and peg_misalign mechanisms (ACCEPTED, verified).** *Slip*: MuJoCo uses the larger friction of two touching geoms unless one has higher priority, so lowering the finger friction alone (as specified) would do nothing; the right finger geoms also get priority 1 for the slip, and both are restored afterwards and at episode end. With scale 0.01 for 20 steps, 100% of cubes drop, a median of 4.5 steps after onset of the slip; at 0.1, 47%. *Nudge*: the trigger radius is measured from the midpoint of the two right finger geoms, which comes within 3.6-6.3 cm of the cube before a grasp. Replays suggested 10 cm and 3 cm, but on the dev review episodes with the live policy only 2/10 failed: the nudge fired at steps 92-119, and the re-query at step 100 (D-15) often saw the moved cube and corrected. Live checks on 10 dev seeds each (runs `nudge_dev_*`): radius 6 cm with a 5 cm shift fired in 8/10 and caused 7 failures, all `grasp_miss` 15-21 steps after the nudge (one regrasp, labeled 157 steps late); 6 cm with 3 cm caused 5/8; 8 cm with 5 cm caused 7/8 with longer lags (28-36 steps). Chosen: 6 cm and 5 cm, which puts the nudge just before the grasp, as H2 assumes. In 2/10 episodes the gripper never came within 6 cm, so the nudge never fired; those episodes are "not fired". The new position is rejected and resampled if it creates a contact. This shows that replayed actions overstate perturbation strength whenever a re-query falls after the perturbation. *peg_misalign*: the peg turns about the axis the fingers close along, around the pinch point, so it stays between the fingers; 10-20 degrees lowers success to 2.5% without dropping the peg (2.5%). All three keep the spec's parameter values except the nudge (radius 6 cm, shift 5 cm).
- **D-20 8x RTX 3070 server runner (ACCEPTED: user request; not yet run on that machine).** `run_3070_server.sh` checks that every selected GPU is an RTX 3070, installs `requirements/server_3070.txt` (the laptop's exact versions, with hashes, for Ubuntu 20.04 or newer) into `~/amon-3070`, runs the full test suite, which includes bit-identical determinism, and then runs `cluster/run_local.sh` with `PROCS_PER_GPU` processes per GPU (default 2) and EGL rendering on each process's own GPU. Tested on the laptop with `ALLOW_OTHER_GPU=1`: install, 43 tests, ready. Server and laptop results are never mixed (D-09). `run_local.sh` now stops after a stage with failing shards instead of running later stages on incomplete data. `fonttools` is held below 4.69: 4.69.0 was yanked on Nov 25 for incomplete provenance and 4.69.1 was a few hours old; neither had been imported.

## Nov 25, 2026 (M0 and M1)

- **D-11 Pinned stack: lerobot 0.3.3, gym-aloha 0.1.1, mujoco 2.3.7, dm-control 1.0.14, torch 2.7.1, Python 3.11 (ACCEPTED).** Chosen before measuring any success rate. lerobot 0.3.3 is the last release that keeps normalization inside the policy, so the March 2025 checkpoints load unmodified with `strict=True`; gym-aloha 0.1.1 is the MuJoCo 2.3 release from the checkpoints' era. Rejected: lerobot 0.4 and later, which moved normalization into processor files these checkpoints do not have (a commenter on issue #2605 reports they cannot be evaluated on main for that reason), so they would need a conversion step that could change the policy under study. `pynput` (teleoperation only) is excluded because it needs kernel headers to build.
- **D-12 Development machine: one RTX 4070 Laptop (8 GB) under WSL2 Ubuntu 22.04, rendering on the AMD 780M through Mesa D3D12 (ACCEPTED).** HANDOFF and D-09 name 8x RTX 3070; this machine is different hardware, so latency measured here cannot stand in for the 3070. The AMD renderer gave bit-identical frames across resets and processes. The NVIDIA D3D12 adapter did not (two resets of one seed hashed differently), and llvmpipe is about 5x slower. Frames differ between the renderers by at most 2 (AMD vs NVIDIA) or 27 (vs llvmpipe) grey levels, with a mean difference under 0.03. The renderer is recorded in `run.json` and counts as hardware under D-09. Any other machine must pass `test_reset_is_deterministic` first.
- **D-13 Episodes end at the first step with reward 4 (ACCEPTED).** This is gym-aloha's own termination and what the published evaluation uses, so T is shorter for successes than for failures (400 steps). Consequences: (a) checks per episode and per-episode alarm rates mix outcome with length, so failures get more checks; (b) anything after success, such as a later drop, is never seen. Rejected for now: padding to a fixed 400 steps, which would change the success definition of the checkpoints. Revisit before M5 if length confounds the budget comparison. Measured in M1: successful transfer_cube episodes last 227-371 steps (median 290) and insertion 259-397 (median 340), against 400 for every failure.
- **D-14 Baseline seeds 1000-1199 (ACCEPTED).** Disjoint from every split, and the first 200 seeds of the published transfer_cube evaluation, whose per-episode outcomes are public, so the comparison is paired. The insertion repository's `eval_info.json` is a copy of transfer_cube's (83.0%), so 20.6% cannot be checked per episode.
- **D-15 Executor is open loop for 100 steps (ACCEPTED for now: replicate the published policy).** Both checkpoints use `n_action_steps = chunk_size = 100` and no temporal ensembling, so the policy looks at an image only at steps 0, 100, 200 and 300. Three consequences for the design: (a) a knock or slip cannot change the executed actions until the next multiple of 100; (b) the disagreement d(t) compares *shadow* chunks, most of which are never executed, so "policy-derived" must be described as needing one extra forward pass per step; (c) `gripper_flip` reads shadow chunks, so it can announce a flip that the executing chunk never performs. Spec section 8 is unchanged; the paper must say this.

## Nov 25, 2026 (scaffold)

- **D-01 Offline replay of schedules (ACCEPTED).** Log every episode once and evaluate every schedule on the same episodes. Valid only while monitors are passive. Rejected: live rollouts per schedule (8× cost, and results would no longer be paired).
- **D-02 Shadow chunk queries (PROPOSED).** Query ACT at every step for logging only; execute per the checkpoint's eval config. This gives a disagreement signal at full rate without changing behavior. Rejected: switching execution to every-step re-query, which would change the policy under study and its success rate.
- **D-03 VLM grid = every 5 steps, 10 Hz (PROPOSED).** Always-on VLM at 50 Hz is too expensive even offline (≈400 calls per episode). Requests between grid points are served at the next grid point, which counts toward delay. Revisit after measuring M3 throughput.
- **D-04 Default world model = frozen DINOv3-S encoder + small trained predictor (PROPOSED).** Rejected as default: end-to-end LeWM-style JEPA, because of collapse risk and its own tuning effort in a 2-week window. Kept as a variant. DINOv2-S is the fallback if DINOv3 weights are gated.
- **D-05 OR-composition trigger (PROPOSED).** The trigger fires if any component fires, with only the disagreement threshold τ tuned to the budget. It is interpretable and makes per-component ablations clean. A weighted sum is an ablation.
- **D-06 Boundary schedule uses privileged sim labels (ACCEPTED, must be disclosed).** A favorable stand-in for an industry success detector at subtask ends: it never misses or delays a boundary. If ours still wins on mid-subtask failures, the result is stronger. Shown with a † in all tables.
- **D-07 Recovered perturbations reported separately (PROPOSED).** Perturbed episodes that still succeed are neither failures nor false-alarm material. An alarm on a real knock is arguably correct even if ACT recovers.
- **D-08 Two environments (ACCEPTED).** `.venv` (lerobot, gym-aloha, mujoco, torch) and `.venv-vlm` (vllm), talking over HTTP. vLLM pins torch.
- **D-09 No mixed hardware within a comparison (ACCEPTED).** Laptop/workstation (8× RTX 3070) and cluster runs are never pooled. Monitor latency used in effective delay is measured on the 3070 for every monitor.
- **D-10 Onset labels from sim predicates (ACCEPTED).** Definitions in spec §4. Validated on video in M2. Unlabeled failures count toward recall only.

## Open questions

- Q1: Exact CARE schedule (events, period, which arm). Read the CARE paper (arXiv 2609.24118) method section before implementing; the "every 50 actions" detail comes from an abstract-level read.
- Q2 (answered, M0): the stages match spec section 1 with these qualifications, from `gym_aloha/tasks/sim.py`. The reward is a stateless function of the current contacts, so it can fall. Each arm is represented by one finger geom (`vx300s_right/10_right_gripper_finger`, `vx300s_left/10_left_gripper_finger`). transfer_cube: 2 is right contact without table contact (a cube knocked off the table also counts as "no table contact"); 3 is left contact, regardless of the right arm or the table; 4 is left contact without table contact, so a normal handover jumps 2 -> 4 and stage 3 appears only when the left arm touches a cube on the table. Measured in M1: all 161 successes went 0(-1)-2-4 and stage 3 never occurred in 200 episodes. insertion: 3 is peg-socket contact with neither on the table, whether or not they are gripped; 4 is peg-pin contact, with no other condition.
- Q5, Q6 (answered, D-16).
- Q7 (ACCEPTED 2026-11-25, implement in M5): perturbed failures without a sim event. 6 of 10 peg_misalign review episodes fail with no drop and no `insert_miss` (stage 3 comes too late for the 150-step window), and one knock is labeled `handover_miss` 163 steps after the push. Spec section 4 makes them `unlabeled` (recall only, no delay). Proposed: for a perturbed failure with no event within 50 steps of the perturbation, set the onset to `perturb_step + 1`, the first frame that shows it, and keep `failure_type` as the perturbation name, so delay is measurable; report them apart from sim-labeled onsets.
- Q8 (ACCEPTED 2026-11-25, implement in M5): a natural failure can precede the perturbation (insertion knock seed 2007: natural drop at step 192, knock at 263). Accepted: a bucket `failed_before_perturbation` (onset < perturb_step), excluded from perturbation metrics like "not fired".
- Q3 (answered, D-18): 20 N throws the cube off the table; the knock is now 5 N, stopped when the grip breaks.
- Q4 (decided: insertion is used for recall and delay only; H3 is tested on transfer_cube): insertion succeeds in 40/200 = 20.0% [15.0, 26.1], so 750 calib rollouts give about 150 successes (113-196), as assumed. The 300 test rollouts give only about 60 successes for false-alarm rates; at a true rate of 0.05 the Wilson interval is then roughly [0.02, 0.14], too wide to test H3 on insertion. 100 of the 160 failures reach stage 3 (peg touches socket) and stop there, which is the weakly labeled `insert_miss` case.
