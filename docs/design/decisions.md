# Design decisions

Dated entries, newest first. Each entry states what was chosen, what was rejected and why. **PROPOSED** entries need the user's confirmation before they produce reported results.

## Nov 25, 2026 (M0 and M1)

- **D-11 Pinned stack: lerobot 0.3.3, gym-aloha 0.1.1, mujoco 2.3.7, dm-control 1.0.14, torch 2.7.1, Python 3.11 (PROPOSED).** Chosen before measuring any success rate. lerobot 0.3.3 is the last release that keeps normalization inside the policy, so the March 2025 checkpoints load unmodified with `strict=True`; gym-aloha 0.1.1 is the MuJoCo 2.3 release from the checkpoints' era. Rejected: lerobot 0.4 and later, which moved normalization into processor files these checkpoints do not have (a commenter on issue #2605 reports they cannot be evaluated on main for that reason), so they would need a conversion step that could change the policy under study. `pynput` (teleoperation only) is excluded because it needs kernel headers to build.
- **D-12 Development machine: one RTX 4070 Laptop (8 GB) under WSL2 Ubuntu 22.04, rendering on the AMD 780M through Mesa D3D12 (PROPOSED).** HANDOFF and D-09 name 8x RTX 3070; this machine is different hardware, so latency measured here cannot stand in for the 3070. The AMD renderer gave bit-identical frames across resets and processes. The NVIDIA D3D12 adapter did not (two resets of one seed hashed differently), and llvmpipe is about 5x slower. Frames differ between the renderers by at most 2 (AMD vs NVIDIA) or 27 (vs llvmpipe) grey levels, with a mean difference under 0.03. The renderer is recorded in `run.json` and counts as hardware under D-09. Any other machine must pass `test_reset_is_deterministic` first.
- **D-13 Episodes end at the first step with reward 4 (PROPOSED).** This is gym-aloha's own termination and what the published evaluation uses, so T is shorter for successes than for failures (400 steps). Consequences: (a) checks per episode and per-episode alarm rates mix outcome with length, so failures get more checks; (b) anything after success, such as a later drop, is never seen. Rejected for now: padding to a fixed 400 steps, which would change the success definition of the checkpoints. Revisit before M5 if length confounds the budget comparison. Measured in M1: successful transfer_cube episodes last 227-371 steps (median 290) and insertion 259-397 (median 340), against 400 for every failure.
- **D-14 Baseline seeds 1000-1199 (ACCEPTED).** Disjoint from every split, and the first 200 seeds of the published transfer_cube evaluation, whose per-episode outcomes are public, so the comparison is paired. The insertion repository's `eval_info.json` is a copy of transfer_cube's (83.0%), so 20.6% cannot be checked per episode.
- **D-15 Executor is open loop for 100 steps (finding, verified).** Both checkpoints use `n_action_steps = chunk_size = 100` and no temporal ensembling, so the policy looks at an image only at steps 0, 100, 200 and 300. Three consequences for the design: (a) a knock or slip cannot change the executed actions until the next multiple of 100; (b) the disagreement d(t) compares *shadow* chunks, most of which are never executed, so "policy-derived" must be described as needing one extra forward pass per step; (c) `gripper_flip` reads shadow chunks, so it can announce a flip that the executing chunk never performs. Spec section 8 is unchanged; the paper must say this.

## Nov 25, 2026 (scaffold)

- **D-01 Offline replay of schedules (ACCEPTED).** Log every episode once and evaluate every schedule on the same episodes. Valid only while monitors are passive. Rejected: live rollouts per schedule (8× cost, and results would no longer be paired).
- **D-02 Shadow chunk queries (PROPOSED).** Query ACT at every step for logging only; execute per the checkpoint's eval config. This gives a disagreement signal at full rate without changing behavior. Rejected: switching execution to every-step re-query, which would change the policy under study and its success rate.
- **D-03 VLM grid = every 5 steps, 10 Hz (PROPOSED).** Always-on VLM at 50 Hz is too expensive even offline (≈400 calls per episode). Requests between grid points are served at the next grid point, which counts toward delay. Revisit after measuring M3 throughput.
- **D-04 Default world model = frozen DINOv3-S encoder + small trained predictor (PROPOSED).** Rejected as default: end-to-end LeWM-style JEPA, because of collapse risk and its own tuning effort in a 2-week window. Kept as a variant. DINOv2-S is the fallback if DINOv3 weights are gated.
- **D-05 OR-composition trigger (PROPOSED).** The trigger fires if any component fires, with only the disagreement threshold τ tuned to the budget. It is interpretable and makes per-component ablations clean. A weighted sum is an ablation.
- **D-06 Boundary schedule uses privileged sim labels (ACCEPTED, must be disclosed).** A favorable stand-in for an industry success detector at subtask ends: it never misses or delays a boundary. If ours still wins on mid-subtask failures, the result is stronger. Shown with a † in all tables.
- **D-07 Recovered perturbations reported separately (PROPOSED).** Perturbed episodes that still succeed are neither failures nor false-alarm material. An alarm on a real knock is arguably correct even if ACT recovers.
- **D-08 Two environments (ACCEPTED).** `.venv` (lerobot, gym-aloha, mujoco, torch) and `.venv-vlm` (vllm), talking over HTTP. vLLM pins torch.
- **D-09 No mixed hardware within a comparison (ACCEPTED).** Laptop/workstation (8× RTX 3070) and cluster runs are never pooled. Monitor latency used in effective delay is measured on the 3070 for every monitor.
- **D-10 Onset labels from sim predicates (ACCEPTED).** Definitions in spec §4. Validated on video in M2. Unlabeled failures count toward recall only.

## Open questions

- Q1: Exact CARE schedule (events, period, which arm). Read the CARE paper (arXiv 2609.24118) method section before implementing; the "every 50 actions" detail comes from an abstract-level read.
- Q2 (answered, M0): the stages match spec section 1 with these qualifications, from `gym_aloha/tasks/sim.py`. The reward is a stateless function of the current contacts, so it can fall. Each arm is represented by one finger geom (`vx300s_right/10_right_gripper_finger`, `vx300s_left/10_left_gripper_finger`). transfer_cube: 2 is right contact without table contact (a cube knocked off the table also counts as "no table contact"); 3 is left contact, regardless of the right arm or the table; 4 is left contact without table contact, so a normal handover jumps 2 -> 4 and stage 3 appears only when the left arm touches a cube on the table. Measured in M1: all 161 successes went 0(-1)-2-4 and stage 3 never occurred in 200 episodes. insertion: 3 is peg-socket contact with neither on the table, whether or not they are gripped; 4 is peg-pin contact, with no other condition.
- Q5 (open, blocks M2 labels): `hold_height_m: 0.02` cannot separate held from resting, because the resting centers are cube 0.0200 m, peg 0.0099 m and socket 0.0219 m. Proposed: held means finger contact and height above `rest_height_m` by a margin, with the margin chosen from the M2 videos.
- Q6 (open, blocks M2 labels): `handover_miss` is defined by "stage 3 not reached after stage 2", but a good handover skips stage 3 (Q2) and ends the episode (D-13). Proposed: "stage >= 3 not reached".
- Q3: Is a knock force of 20 N enough to dislodge the cube without launching it? Tune on 10 videos in M2.
- Q4 (measured in M1, scope is the user's decision): insertion succeeds in 40/200 = 20.0% [15.0, 26.1], so 750 calib rollouts give about 150 successes (113-196), as assumed. The 300 test rollouts give only about 60 successes for false-alarm rates; at a true rate of 0.05 the Wilson interval is then roughly [0.02, 0.14], too wide to test H3 on insertion. 100 of the 160 failures reach stage 3 (peg touches socket) and stop there, which is the weakly labeled `insert_miss` case.
